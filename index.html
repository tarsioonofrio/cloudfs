GDisk

Your Google Drive as a normal folder on Linux.

GDisk is a personal, independent Linux client that mounts a Google Drive account as a local folder, using FUSE. Once mounted, you browse, open, create, edit, rename, move and delete your Drive files with any regular program (file manager, terminal, editor), without opening a web browser.

What it does

  • Mounts your Drive as a directory. Files are downloaded on demand, only the parts you read.
  • Caches on your computer the metadata and the file content you opened, so repeated reads are fast.
  • Uploads your changes back to Drive: new files, edits, renames, moves and deletions (deleted files go to the Drive trash).
  • Stays in sync by polling the Drive changes feed, so edits made elsewhere show up locally.
  • Shows Google Docs, Sheets and Slides as small .gdoc shortcut files that point to the document.
  • Offline pinning of files you want to keep in the local cache.

How you sign in

You run gdrived login, which opens the standard Google sign-in page in your browser and asks for permission to access your Drive. Sign-in and consent are handled by Google; GDisk never sees your Google password. The resulting token is stored only on your own computer.

Your data stays on your computer

GDisk runs entirely on your machine. It talks directly to the Google Drive API. There is no GDisk server: nothing about you or your files is sent to the developer or to any third party. See the Privacy Policy for details.

Why it asks for access to Drive

To behave as a file system, GDisk must list, read, create, modify, move and trash the files in your Drive, which requires the Google Drive scope https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive. It uses that access only to do what you ask through the mounted folder.

GDisk's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
